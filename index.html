How the Driver File Audit works

Every audit follows one written checklist, built item by item from 49 CFR Part 391 (driver files, including the list in 391.51) and Part 382 (drug and alcohol testing). FMCSA's safety audit for new carriers looks at both areas, among others (49 CFR 385.311).

The full checklist has 29 items. The main ones, for each driver:

  • A signed job application.
  • Driving records from hiring and every 12 months, and a note of each yearly review.
  • Previous employers' safety history for the past 3 years, or a note of your good-faith effort to get it.
  • A road test certificate, or the license you accepted instead.
  • The medical card, or for CDL drivers, the state record showing medical status.
  • For CDL drivers: a pre-employment drug test result and Clearinghouse queries, on file.

You get a written report, item by item: what's in the file, what's missing, and what's out of date.

Federal rules keep drug and alcohol test records private between you and your drivers (49 CFR 382.405), so I never see them. For those items you check each one on my list and tell me "on file" or "not on file." You still get the full list, what's missing, and the written report.

  • My notes list drivers by number, never by name.
  • It's information, not legal advice, and I can't promise how any safety audit goes.
  • Up to 90 minutes for 1 to 5 drivers, about 2 hours for 6 to 10, longer for bigger fleets.
